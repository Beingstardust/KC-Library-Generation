# 71. INT-22 and INT-22b: prose with another sentence's mathematics spliced through it

Commits `c1cf31d` and `4e36545`. Measurements are in the commit messages; this is the reasoning
and the two lessons that outlast the change.

## The defect

The external content review's clearest mathematical finding was Group Average Linkage stating that
WPGMA's coefficients are "constants independent of the cluster sizes" and then giving coefficients
built from cluster sizes. The cause was in the corpus, not the drafter:

```
mineru  : "...clusters, A and B that werem A m B merged: For the weightedalphaA=mA/(mA+mB),
           alphaB=mB/(mA+mB), beta=0, gamma=0 version of group average - known as WPGMA - the
           coefficients are constants that are independent of the cluster sizes:"
docling : "For the weighted version of group average-known as WPGMA-the coefficients are
           constants that are independent of the cluster sizes:"
```

One extractor splices marginal display equations into the middle of the running text. UPGMA's
coefficients land inside the sentence that introduces WPGMA. No drafter reading that row could bind
the right formula to the right name, and the review was reading a genuine corpus corruption.

## The repair, and the one discriminator that makes it possible

This is INT-11's idea - another extractor rendered the same content cleanly, so use that one -
applied to prose rather than to formulas. The hard part is detection, and it comes down to a single
property:

> the interrupted run must be a real word the corpus uses on its own

A first signature without that condition flagged 279 rows, nearly all of them ordinary notation:
`dN(p,c)`, `g-lambda`, `Mui-alpha`. Requiring the run to be at least four letters AND to appear on
its own at least three times elsewhere in the corpus takes it to 109. "weighted" is a word; "Mui"
is not. Everything else in the mechanism is a safety condition on top of that one discriminator.

Two further conditions, each guarded: the twin must come from a DIFFERENT extractor, or it is the
same rendering rather than an independent one; and a row with no clean twin is left ALONE rather
than dropped. The second is the lesson from doc 58's bar-loss signature, where 100% precision was
still not a reason to ship - detection without repair loses the sentence entirely, which is worse
than keeping a damaged one.

## INT-22b: I committed an assumption without checking it

INT-22 replaced a damaged row with a clean twin whenever one existed. The clean twin does not carry
the spliced equation - that is the point, the equation belonged to a different sentence - and the
repair ASSUMED it survives elsewhere in the corpus. For Group Average Linkage it does. I shipped
that assumption unverified.

It does not always hold. Chi-Squared shows the shape at its worst: `(number of rows -1)x(number of
columns-1)` exists ONLY inside a spliced sentence, because the row meant to carry it reads "the
general formula for the degrees of freedom is df= ." with the formula missing. Repairing such a row
would delete the general form from the corpus outright. When measured, **20 of 83 repairs deleted
mathematics found nowhere else**, including `Z_alpha/2 = Z_1-alpha/2` and `N = sum_i n_i`.

INT-22b adds the condition: every mathematical fragment the clean rendering drops must appear in at
least one other text, or the damaged row is left alone. 80 repairs over 86 rows, down from 83 over
89. Group Average Linkage is still repaired.

**The lesson is not "check your assumptions" but something narrower and more useful: a repair that
substitutes one rendering for another is a DELETION of whatever only the original carried.** Any
future substitution rule in this pipeline needs the same non-destructive check, and it needs it
before shipping rather than after.

## Getting the fragment comparison right took four corrections

Each was found by a failing guard, not by inspection, and all four are the same kind of error -
a text-normalisation detail that makes two renderings of the same mathematics compare unequal:

```
Python's \w matches Greek     the naive fragment carried the word the splice glued to it
                              ("weightedalphaA=...") and so was unique BY CONSTRUCTION, which
                              blocked every repair including the one this work exists for
trailing punctuation          "alphaA=mA/(mA+mB)" vs "alphaA=mA/(mA+mB)," read as different
the first fix for that        stripped a closing paren belonging to the expression itself
INT22-1's own fixture         then correctly refused its repair: in a corpus of two rows that
                              mathematics really IS unique. It now carries a third row.
```

The first is the one to remember. A uniqueness test whose subject is contaminated by the very
damage it is testing for will report everything as unique and silently disable the whole mechanism.
It failed CLOSED, which is why a guard caught it rather than the corpus quietly going unrepaired.

## Sabotage found two more defects in the guards

INT22-7 passed for the wrong reason after INT-22b landed: with the same-extractor rule removed the
repair was still refused, but by INT-22b's non-destructive condition, because the fixture's tiny
corpus made the spliced mathematics unique. The guard held whether or not the rule it names
existed. Fixed by giving the fixture an elsewhere row.

The first sabotage case also lost its anchor to INT-22b's rewrite and reported "anchor not found" -
a sabotage that tests nothing while looking like it ran. This is the failure mode to watch for
whenever a commit rewrites code that an existing sabotage case targets by text.

## A correction this change forced

INT-22 rewrites the corpus, so the BM25 index is rebuilt over different text and scores shift
globally. Comparing r6 to r7 on admitted evidence text gives 33 units changed; comparing whole
packets gives 152 of 159. Every A/B in this audit reported the former. The correctness conclusions
stand, but see doc 72 for the corrected figures and the reporting rule that follows from them.
